# 02 — Standards, regulation and deployment requirements

What applies to an iPad controller for a Unitree G1 humanoid in a surgical wing, and what each item means for the app as design rules.

> **Deployment country is not fixed.** The project is at NTU, Singapore, so Singapore items (HSA, PDPA, CSA, Synapxe/cluster IT, SS standards) are listed alongside US (FDA, HIPAA) and EU (MDR, GDPR, Machinery Regulation, AI Act) equivalents. Pick one jurisdiction before any clinical use; the design rules below are written to satisfy all three.

## 0. How to read "applies"

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Applies** | Applies to this product as used in a hospital, or will once it is used with patients or staff at work |
| **Conditional** | Applies only if the intended purpose makes the robot (or the app) a medical device, or the product is placed on that market |
| **By analogy** | Does not legally apply. It is the best available engineering practice for the problem, and we follow it on purpose |

**Current status.** A simulation-first research prototype (MuJoCo / Isaac). Nothing is used on patients. Everything below is what the app must be *ready for*. The cheapest time to build it in is now.

## 1. Regulatory position (the question to settle first)

| Question | Analysis | Consequence for the app |
|---|---|---|
| Is the G1 + app a **medical device**? | Depends on intended purpose. A robot that *carries* items (like TUG, Moxi, SGH Swiftie) is generally treated as hospital logistics equipment, not a medical device. A robot that **takes part in surgery** by selecting and handing instruments to the surgeon in the sterile field is much closer to one. It is at least an accessory, and arguably "surgical equipment", and regulators may view it that way. EU MDR: software that "drives or influences the use of a hardware medical device" follows the device, and the intended purpose decides qualification (MDCG 2019-11). HSA (Singapore): software is classified with the device it drives (GL-04 software life-cycle guidelines, rev. 2025). FDA: device definition + De Novo route for novel robots (e.g. Ottava). | **Conditional**, and needs a formal regulatory determination before any theatre trial. Until then, **design as if regulated**: IEC 62304 software safety Class B (C for anything commanding motion near people), IEC 62366-1 usability file, ISO 14971 risk file. |
| Is it **surgical robot equipment** (IEC 80601-2-77 RASE)? | 2-77 covers robotically assisted surgical equipment, where the robot acts on the patient under surgeon control. The G1 does **not** manipulate the patient. | **By analogy**: borrow its ideas (clear indication of which arm is active, surgeon awareness of robot motion, recovery from interruptions). |
| Is it a **personal care / service robot** (ISO 13482)? | ISO 13482:2014 *excludes* "robots as medical devices" and covers mobile servant robots in human environments. The revision (ISO/FDIS 13482, "service robots") widens the scope. A logistics humanoid in corridors is the closest fit. | **Applies by analogy now**; likely **applies** to the non-surgical functions (portering, corridor navigation) once published. |
| **Industrial / collaborative robot** (ISO 10218-1/-2:2025, ex-ISO/TS 15066) | Written for industrial robots. 2025 editions fold in collaborative applications, mode requirements and **cybersecurity**. | **By analogy** for arm speed/force near people (power and force limiting), enabling-device and mode-selection rules. |
| **Dynamically stable robots** (ISO 25785-1, draft) | First standard for legged/humanoid robots that need active control to stay upright. Removing power → fall. Draft, led by A3/Agility/Boston Dynamics, expected 2026–27. | **By analogy, most relevant.** Drives rule S-1 (stop ≠ power off). |
| **Machinery** (EU 2023/1230, from 20 Jan 2027) | Covers autonomous mobile machinery, self-evolving (ML) behaviour and safety-relevant cybersecurity. | **Conditional (EU)**. The "Teach a task" feature is self-evolving behaviour: approved behaviour must be versioned and frozen (rule T-1). |
| **Research / clinical investigation** | Singapore: HSA does not regulate device clinical trials as such, but devices used for a clinical purpose in research are "clinical research material". An IRB (e.g. SingHealth CIRB, NHG DSRB) plus hospital approvals are needed. US: IDE / IRB. EU: MDR Art. 62+ clinical investigation. | App must support **study mode**: consent-aware recording, de-identified exports, protocol version shown. |

Singapore note: CSA's **Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme for Medical Devices, CLS(MD)** (voluntary, Oct 2024, 4 levels) is run jointly with MOH, HSA and **Synapxe** (the public-healthcare IT agency). Public-cluster procurement increasingly expects it. Target Level 2 if the robot becomes a medical device.

## 2. Requirement → source → design rule

Rule IDs are referenced from `04` and `05`.

### 2.1 Safety and stopping

| ID | Requirement | Source | What it means for the app (concrete rule) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-1 | A humanoid is not safe when unpowered. Stopping must keep it balanced unless a fall is the lesser harm. | ISO 25785-1 (draft) concept of actively controlled stability; Agility Digit Category 1 stop; Unitree: damping → "slowly fall to the ground"; Spot guide: don't STOP on stairs | Two distinct controls. **STOP** = protective hold: cancel nav/mission, zero velocity, **freeze arms where they are** (do *not* blend to default), stay standing (mode → STAND). **Power-off (DAMP)** = robot sinks/falls; for "it is falling / hurting someone / out of control". Backend change needed: today `estop` = DAMP, and leaving MANIP blends arms to default over ~1 s. That motion must not happen on STOP during a handover. |
| S-2 | Emergency-stop devices need a red actuator on a yellow background, mechanical latching and direct-opening contacts, with Category 0 or 1 stop. Red/yellow is reserved for e-stop. | ISO 13850:2015; IEC 60204-1 stop categories; NFPA 79 (touchscreen e-stops not allowed) | The iPad button **is not an emergency stop** and must not look like one. **No red-on-yellow mushroom styling.** Label it "Stop" and "Power off robot". A **hardware wireless e-stop** (latching, Category 1 on the G1, plus the Unitree remote's damping chord) is mandatory for every session. The app's start-up check requires the hardware e-stop heartbeat to be green before any motion mode is allowed. |
| S-3 | Stop must always be available and never obscured. | IEC 60204-1 §10.7 (principle); ISO 13850 accessibility | **Stop is visible on every screen, every mode, every role, including modals, keyboards and the lock screen**, in a fixed position (safety rail), ≥ 88 × 88 pt (≈ 17 mm), one tap, no confirmation, works with gloves, and is never covered by toasts, sheets or popovers (z-index above everything; modals are inset so the rail stays live). |
| S-4 | Reset/resume after a stop must be deliberate, and must not by itself restart motion. | ISO 13850 §4.1.4 (reset shall not initiate restart) | After STOP: robot holds. The app shows "Stopped by Nurse Tan, 10:42 — reason?" and a **Resume** that states exactly what will happen ("Continue to Theatre 1 — 12 m"), with a slide-to-confirm. Resume never happens automatically, including after reconnection. |
| S-5 | Hold-to-run / enabling device for manual motion near people. | ISO 10218-1:2025 (enabling device, reduced speed in manual mode) | Manual locomotion and arm teleop use **press-and-hold**: releasing the joystick or the "hold to move" pad sends zero at once. Default manual speed cap 0.3 m/s in clinical areas (contract allows 0.8). Clinical-area speed profile set per zone by technician. |
| S-6 | Single point of control. | Spot lease model; ISO 10218 mode selection (one control location at a time) | **Control lease**: exactly one iPad (or the keyboard node) is "in control" for manual modes. Others see "Observing — controlled by iPad OR-2 (S. Tan)". Taking control needs a tap + reason, is logged, and alerts the previous holder. **Stop works from every connected device regardless of lease.** |
| S-7 | Loss of communication → safe state. | Spot (3 s → sit, 8 s → power off); ISO 10218 | In LOCO/MANIP: if the controlling iPad misses its 4 Hz heartbeat for **> 1.0 s** → STAND (hold). NAV/AUTO continue, but an alert is raised if no iPad is connected for > 10 s. The app shows a connection-quality meter; below threshold, manual modes cannot start. (Backend: manager watchdog on a `/g1/ui/heartbeat` topic.) |
| S-8 | Robot motion in shared space must be visible to bystanders. | ISO 13482 (information for use, warning of motion); IEC 80601-2-77 (indication of motion) | The app mirrors a **robot-body light** state and uses spoken phrases ("Moving to Theatre 1", "Arms moving"). App setting: speak before moving arms in AUTO when a person is detected within 1 m. |

### 2.2 Usability engineering

| ID | Requirement | Source | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| U-1 | Usability engineering process: use specification, user interface specification, known use problems, hazard-related use scenarios, formative evaluation, summative evaluation. | **IEC 62366-1:2015+A1:2020**; IEC 60601-1-6 (points ME equipment to 62366-1); FDA "Applying Human Factors and Usability Engineering to Medical Devices" (2016) | `04_user_groups_and_requirements.md` is our **use specification**. Keep a **critical-task list**: stop the robot, send to the right room, request the right instrument, take over manually, recover from a fault. Each critical task gets formative tests with real theatre nurses (≥ 5 per role per round) before any summative study (15+ per user group, per FDA). |
| U-2 | Critical tasks are those whose failure could cause serious harm. Evaluate them under realistic conditions. | FDA HF guidance | Test with gloves, in dim theatre light, at 1–2 m, with noise playback (70 dB(A) with 90 dB peaks) and interruptions. The app must support a **"test harness" mode** that records tap targets and times, with no PHI. |
| U-3 | Mode awareness. The user must always know the active mode and what the robot will do next. | IEC 62366-1 (use errors); aviation mode-error literature; IEC 60601-1-8 (indicator conspicuity) | **Mode ribbon** on every screen: icon + word + colour ("Navigating · to Theatre 1 · 8 m"). A mode change is announced visually (300 ms sweep) and audibly (short earcon) and spoken by the robot. See `03` §4. |
| U-4 | Information for safety must be legible at the operator position. | IEC 60601-1-8: visual alarm legible at 1 m | Hero status text ≥ 44 pt; across-room view ≥ 88 pt (derivation in `05`). |

### 2.3 Alarms

**IEC 60601-1-8 applies by analogy.** The G1 is not physiological-monitoring ME equipment, but theatre staff are trained on its conventions, and deviating from them causes misreads.

| ID | Requirement | Source | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-1 | Three alarm priorities with fixed visual coding: **High = red, flashing 1.4–2.8 Hz**, 20–60% duty; **Medium = yellow, flashing 0.4–0.8 Hz**; **Low = cyan or yellow, steady**. | IEC 60601-1-8 Table 1 | Use exactly this: High red flashing 2 Hz (50% duty), Medium amber 0.6 Hz, Low cyan steady. **Red and flashing are reserved for High alarms.** Nothing else in the UI flashes, and red is used nowhere else except the Stop control. The Stop button is red but steady. |
| A-2 | Priority is set by the harm and how fast it could happen. | IEC 60601-1-8 §6.1.2 | Mapping in `04` §7. Example: robot fall / collision / arm moving near sterile field unexpectedly = High; stuck > 60 s on a timed task, battery < 15% mid-task, localisation lost = Medium; battery < 30% idle, map drift, camera frames stale = Low. |
| A-3 | Auditory alarm signals distinguish priority (classic pattern: High = 10-pulse burst repeated, Medium = 3 pulses, Low = 1–2 pulses). The 2020 amendment adds **auditory icons** (meaningful sounds) + pointers. | IEC 60601-1-8:2006+A2:2020 | Three earcon families. The robot's own speaker is the **primary** audible channel (iPad audio is unreliable; see `05` §7). The iPad plays the same patterns only when it is the controlling device. Never use a High pattern for non-High events. |
| A-4 | Alarm states: active, **acknowledged**, **audio paused** (timed), alarm off (restricted). Latching vs non-latching. Indicate inactivation. | IEC 60601-1-8 §6.8 | Clinical users can **acknowledge** (silences for 2 min; re-sounds if still true) but cannot disable. Technician can suppress a Low alarm class for the session, with a visible "1 alarm suppressed" chip. High alarms latch until acknowledged, even if the condition clears ("Robot fell 10:42 — acknowledged by …"). |
| A-5 | Alarm fatigue: 85–99% of clinical alarms need no action. | The Joint Commission, Sentinel Event Alert #50 (2013); NPSG.06.01.01 | **Budget**: target < 1 non-actionable alarm per theatre case. Every alarm must state **what happened, what to do, who should do it**. Informational events are *not* alarms: they go to the timeline without sound. |

### 2.4 Software life cycle and security

| ID | Requirement | Source | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-1 | Software life cycle, safety classification, SOUP management, problem resolution. | **IEC 62304:2006+A1:2015** (conditional; design as if Class B/C) | App version, robot software version, policy version and map version shown in Technician → About and embedded in every incident export. Web dependencies pinned and listed (SOUP list) — current vanilla-JS, no-CDN approach is ideal. |
| L-2 | Security activities in the product life cycle (threat model, secure coding, security testing, vulnerability handling). | **IEC 81001-5-1:2021** (EU harmonised; FDA recognised); FDA "Cybersecurity in Medical Devices" final guidance (June 2025, implements **§524B**: SBOM, vulnerability monitoring, patching) | Threat model the WebSocket API. **Today it is unauthenticated and plain `ws://`; that must change before any hospital network.** Provide an SBOM for the robot + UI server. |
| L-3 | Authentication and authorisation for every safety-relevant command. | IEC 81001-5-1; ISO 10218-1:2025 cybersecurity clause; EU 2023/1230 Annex III 1.1.9 (protection against corruption) | Every WS op carries a session token bound to user + role + device. The server enforces the permission matrix (`04` §5). The UI only hides buttons; the **server refuses**. Stop is always accepted from any authenticated device. |
| L-4 | Transport security. | Same | HTTPS/WSS only, served from the robot (or an on-prem edge box) with a certificate from the **hospital's internal CA**, pushed to iPads by MDM. No mixed content. Several Safari APIs (Wake Lock, speech, getUserMedia) need a secure context anyway. |
| L-5 | Network segregation; no vendor cloud. | SingHealth COI report (2019) → **Internet Surfing Separation** in Singapore public healthcare; network segmentation; UniPwn (G1 BLE root exploit, 2025) and observed G1 telemetry to vendor servers | Robot + iPads on a **dedicated medical-device VLAN/SSID**, no internet egress (block the G1's vendor telemetry at the firewall), **BLE provisioning disabled / out of range**. Technician view shows a "Network posture" card: VLAN OK, internet egress blocked, BLE off, cert valid until …. |
| L-6 | Audit logging and accountability. | IEC 81001-5-1; Relay chain-of-custody; PDPA accountability | Append-only audit log on the robot: user, role, device, op, args, result, mode before/after, time. Viewable by technician/manager and exportable. The existing `/rosout` log is not an audit log. |
| L-7 | Updates. | FDA §524B (patchability); da Vinci 5 network updates | Technician-only "Software" screen: current versions, staged update, **never update during a scheduled theatre session**, rollback. |

### 2.5 Privacy and cameras

| ID | Requirement | Source | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-1 | Images of identifiable people are personal data. Notification and consent are needed unless an exception applies. In non-public areas (theatres, staff rooms) expect **explicit** consent and signage. Individuals have access rights to footage. | **PDPA (Singapore)**, PDPC Advisory Guidelines on Selected Topics (CCTV/photography chapter, rev. 2024) | Camera views (OBSBOT operator camera, G1 head RGB-D) are **live-only by default: no recording, no snapshots** in Clinical mode. Recording is an Operator/Technician action with an on-screen **REC indicator on every device** and an announcement by the robot. Theatre signage template in deployment kit. |
| P-2 | Patient images in theatre are health information. | **HIPAA** Privacy/Security Rules (US) — full-face photos are a PHI identifier; **GDPR** Art. 9 (EU) special-category data | Blur faces by default in any stored frame. Store no PHI on the iPad (no local storage/IndexedDB of images). Recording retention is set by hospital policy, default 0 days (not kept). Research recordings (teach demonstrations) need IRB consent and are exported de-identified. |
| P-3 | Minimise. | PDPA purpose limitation; GDPR data minimisation | The instrument-request flow never needs patient identity. **The app has no patient fields at all.** The theatre/case is identified by room + time only. |
| P-4 | Staff monitoring. | PDPA; employment/union context (the Moxi experience) | Usage analytics are aggregated by role and room, **not** per named nurse, except in the audit log (access-controlled, for incident review only). State this in onboarding. |

### 2.6 Infection prevention and sterile field

| ID | Requirement | Source | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| I-1 | Non-sterile personnel stay ≥ 30 cm (12 in) from the sterile field and never reach over it. | AORN Guideline for Sterile Technique; AST sterile field guidelines | The **iPad is non-sterile and never enters the sterile field**. Mount it on the circulator's side (IV-pole or wall arm) ≥ 1 m from the table. **Scrubbed staff and the surgeon never touch it.** They interact by voice (via the circulator now, the robot's own voice interface later) and by glance. Place the robot so that its instrument hand approaches from the scrub side. That is a robot-behaviour rule the app shows in the mission view. |
| I-2 | Low-level disinfection of touchscreens between cases; tablets carry pathogens (MRSA found on 64% of inpatient-use iPads in one study). | Apple support (70% isopropyl or Clorox wipes allowed; no bleach/H₂O₂); infection-control literature | **"Clean screen" mode**: 30 s touch lock with a countdown, reachable from the lock screen and the end-of-case flow (Stop stays active via a 2-s long-press only). Use a sealed, wipeable medical case (IP65, no seams), and no screen protector with edges that trap fluid. Log cleaning in the end-of-shift checklist. Confirm the hospital's disinfectant (e.g. quaternary ammonium / CHG / alcohol) is compatible with the case. |
| I-3 | Gloved use. | Nitrile/latex gloves ≤ 1 mm usually work on capacitive screens, but less precisely | Every clinical control ≥ 64 pt (≈ 12 mm); no swipe-only actions; no long-press as the only path; no pinch-zoom required. Double-gloved scrub hands are not a supported input. |

### 2.7 Accessibility

| ID | Requirement | Source | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| X-1 | Target size ≥ 24 × 24 CSS px (AA); 44 × 44 (AAA). | WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 / 2.5.5 | Exceed both: clinical ≥ 64 pt, operator ≥ 48 pt, technician ≥ 44 pt. |
| X-2 | Colour is not the only means of conveying information. | WCAG 1.4.1 | Every status = colour + icon + word. Red-green colour-vision deficiency: ~8% of Caucasian males, **~5% of Singapore Chinese males**, ~0.2–0.5% of females. |
| X-3 | Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 text, ≥ 3:1 UI components and graphics. | WCAG 1.4.3 / 1.4.11 | All tokens in `05` are specified with contrast in both light and theatre-dark. |
| X-4 | Motion. | WCAG 2.3.1 (no > 3 flashes/s) / 2.3.3 | The 2 Hz High-alarm flash is under 3/s and limited to the alarm banner area; honour `prefers-reduced-motion` for everything else. |
| X-5 | Language. | Singapore: English is the working language, and theatre teams are multinational (Filipino, Indian, Malaysian, Myanmar nurses are common) | Plain English, short verbs, no idioms; icons carry meaning. Strings externalised for later Chinese/Malay/Tamil. Robot speech in the same phrases. |

### 2.8 iPad deployment (MDM)

| ID | Requirement | Source | Design rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-1 | Supervised, MDM-managed iPads (Jamf Pro / Intune / Workspace ONE; in Singapore public healthcare, via the cluster's IT / Synapxe standards). | Apple Platform Deployment; Jamf | Deliver as a **Home Screen web app (web clip)**, or better, a thin **native WKWebView wrapper**. The wrapper allows Autonomous Single App Mode, managed app configuration (robot URL, room, device name), and certificate pinning. Plain Safari in Single App Mode is the fallback. |
| D-2 | Kiosk locking. | Single App Mode (MDM), Guided Access (local, passcode) | Theatre iPads locked to the app. Guided Access is not enough on its own, because a user can exit it with the passcode. |
| D-3 | Screen must not sleep while controlling. | Screen Wake Lock API (Safari 16.4+; fixed for Home Screen apps in iOS 18.4) | Request a wake lock while in any motion mode or with a live alarm. Also set MDM auto-lock to "Never" for theatre devices on a charging mount. |
| D-4 | Identity. | Shared-device reality | **Badge tap** (NFC reader on the mount, or the robot's badge reader) or a 4–6 digit PIN to switch role. Auto-drop to Clinical after 10 min idle; Technician after 5 min. |
| D-5 | Device health. | – | Status bar shows iPad battery (via `navigator.getBattery` is **not** available in Safari → use MDM-reported battery or the mount's charger state), connection and the time since the last robot message. |

## 3. What does *not* apply (and why we still care)

| Item | Why not | Why we still borrow from it |
|---|---|---|
| IEC 80601-2-77 (RASE) | The G1 does not act on the patient | Motion indication, recovery after interruption, team awareness of robot state |
| IEC 60601-1-8 (alarms) | Not physiological ME equipment | Staff are trained on its colour and sound language (A-1…A-5) |
| ISO 10218 / TS 15066 | Not an industrial robot | Enabling device, speed limits in manual mode, force limits for handover |
| ISO 13850 as an iPad feature | A touchscreen cannot be an e-stop | It dictates that a hardware e-stop exists and that the iPad must not pretend to be one |
| ISO 13482:2014 | Excludes medical devices | Mobile-servant-robot risks (collision, fall, unexpected start) |
| EU AI Act | Only if placed on the EU market; a safety component of MDR/Machinery products would be high-risk | Logging, human oversight and transparency are good design regardless |
